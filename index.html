🔍 Problem Orientation

Imagine this: You are using your phone to send a message. The signal travels through invisible waves. But how does it work?

Driving Question: "How do waves transfer energy and how do they benefit human life?"

In this lesson, you will act as a young scientist to investigate how waves behave and how they are used in daily life such as communication, medicine, and energy.

🧠 Investigation (Concept Building)

What is a Wave?

A wave is a way in which energy moves from one place to another without transferring matter.

Types of Waves:

  • 🌊 Mechanical Waves (need medium, e.g., sound waves)
  • 💡 Electromagnetic Waves (no medium, e.g., light, Wi-Fi)

Wave Characteristics:

  • Amplitude → height of wave (energy)
  • Wavelength → distance between peaks
  • Frequency → number of waves per second

🧪 Experiment (Hands-on Activity)

Try this simple experiment at home:

  1. Take a rope or slinky
  2. Move one end up and down
  3. Observe how the wave travels

Questions to think about:

  • Does the rope move forward?
  • What actually travels?

👉 Conclusion: Only energy moves, not the object!

🌍 Waves, Light & Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs)

Waves and light are not only scientific concepts but also play an important role in achieving global sustainability goals.

⚡ SDG 7: Affordable and Clean Energy

Light waves from the sun are used in solar panels to produce renewable energy. This helps reduce dependence on fossil fuels and supports clean energy development.

🏗️ SDG 9: Industry, Innovation, and Infrastructure

Electromagnetic waves such as radio waves, microwaves, and infrared waves are used in communication technologies like Wi-Fi, mobile phones, and satellites. These technologies support global connectivity and innovation.

🌊 SDG 13: Climate Action

Waves are used in environmental monitoring systems. For example, satellite imaging uses electromagnetic waves to observe climate change, weather patterns, and natural disasters.

🏥 SDG 3: Good Health and Well-being

Light waves are used in medical technologies such as X-rays, lasers, and imaging tools that help diagnose and treat diseases.

🌱 Blue & Green Curriculum

🔵 Blue Curriculum

Focuses on ocean and water sustainability. By learning about waves, students understand:

  • Ocean wave energy
  • Coastal processes
  • Impact of waves on marine ecosystems

🟢 Green Curriculum

Focuses on environmental awareness and sustainability. Through wave learning:

  • Students learn renewable energy (solar energy)
  • Understand environmental impact of technology
  • Develop eco-friendly thinking

👉 By combining science and sustainability, students are encouraged to become responsible global citizens.
